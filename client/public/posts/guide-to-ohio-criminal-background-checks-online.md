#### **What Information is Included in an Ohio Criminal Background Check?**

An Ohio criminal background check typically includes a variety of information regarding a person’s criminal history. The specifics can vary depending on the type of background check being conducted, but here are the primary elements you can expect to find:

Felony convictions are the most serious crimes in Ohio and can have long-lasting consequences for an individual’s personal and professional life. A criminal background check will reveal any felony charges or convictions, including details on the offense, the date it occurred, and the sentence handed down.

##### **2\. Misdemeanors**

Misdemeanors are less serious offenses compared to felonies but can still impact an individual’s reputation and employment prospects. A criminal background check will list any misdemeanors that a person has been convicted of, including the type of crime and the sentence received.

##### **3\. Pending Charges**

In addition to past convictions, a background check can also reveal any pending criminal charges. These are charges for crimes that have been filed but not yet resolved, and they can provide insight into potential future legal issues an individual may face.

##### **4\. Arrest Records**

Arrest records can also appear in a criminal background check, although they do not necessarily indicate a conviction. An arrest record simply shows that an individual was apprehended by law enforcement, and while it may not result in a conviction, it can still be a significant factor when evaluating a candidate for employment, housing, or other purposes.

##### **5\. Sex Offender Registry**

If an individual is listed on the Ohio sex offender registry, this information will be included in a criminal background check. This is crucial for employers, landlords, and others who may need to know about the potential risks an individual poses to vulnerable populations.

#### **Legal Implications of Ohio Criminal Background Checks**

![Legal Implications of Ohio Criminal Background Checks](https://precisehire.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Legal-Implications-of-Ohio-Criminal-Background-Checks.png)

Criminal background checks are a valuable tool for employers, landlords, and individuals in Ohio, but they come with a set of legal responsibilities. Understanding these responsibilities is vital to ensure that background checks are conducted in compliance with state and federal laws. Some key legal frameworks and concerns include:

##### **1\. Fair Credit Reporting Act \(FCRA\)**

The **Fair Credit Reporting Act \(FCRA\)** is a federal law that regulates the use of consumer information, including criminal background checks. If you are an employer conducting background checks as part of your hiring process, the FCRA requires that you:

  * Obtain the **written consent** of the candidate before running a criminal background check.
  * Notify the candidate if you intend to use the background check to make an adverse decision \(e.g., not hiring the candidate based on their criminal record\).
  * Provide the candidate with a copy of the background check and an opportunity to dispute any inaccuracies if you decide not to hire them based on the results.

Failure to comply with the FCRA can result in legal actions, so it is essential to ensure that you follow the law when using criminal background checks in employment decisions.

##### **2\. Ohio Revised Code \(ORC\)**

In Ohio, the **Ohio Revised Code \(ORC\)** also governs criminal background checks, with specific provisions that must be followed for both employers and individuals. Some important points include:

  * Employers in Ohio are prohibited from asking about a candidate’s criminal history on the initial job application under the **“ Ban the Box”** law. This law prevents discrimination based on criminal records at the early stages of the hiring process.
  * Ohio law requires that background checks for certain types of jobs \(e.g., healthcare, government positions\) are more stringent, and employers may have to obtain more comprehensive reports.

##### **3\. Discrimination and Equal Employment Opportunity \(EEO\)**

Employers must be cautious about how they use criminal background checks to avoid discrimination based on race, national origin, gender, or other protected categories. The **Equal Employment Opportunity Commission \(EEOC\)** has issued guidelines that suggest employers should conduct background checks in a manner that does not disproportionately affect individuals from certain demographic groups.

Additionally, it’s important to note that Ohio does not allow employers to automatically disqualify individuals with certain types of convictions, especially if they occurred many years ago. Employers must consider the relevance of the criminal history to the specific job in question before making any adverse hiring decisions.

##### **4\. Privacy Concerns**

While criminal background checks are an essential part of ensuring safety and compliance, they also raise privacy concerns. It’s crucial to balance the need for background checks with the protection of an individual’s privacy rights. In Ohio, this means ensuring that:

  * The person being checked has provided consent.
  * The information is not used for discriminatory purposes.
  * Data collected during the background check is securely stored and handled.
