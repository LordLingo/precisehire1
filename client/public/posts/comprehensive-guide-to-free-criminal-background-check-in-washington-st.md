### **Introduction to Criminal Background Checks in Washington State**

In Washington State, a **criminal background check** is an essential tool used to evaluate an individual’s criminal history for a variety of purposes. Whether for **employment** , **housing applications** , **volunteer positions** , or **licensing requirements** , understanding how to obtain and interpret these checks is crucial.

A criminal background check allows employers, landlords, and other organizations to verify the criminal history of an individual to ensure they meet the necessary qualifications and standards. In Washington State, individuals can access criminal background information in several ways, but the availability and accuracy of this information depend on whether the check is free or paid.

#### **What is a Criminal Background Check?**

A **criminal background check** is a process of reviewing an individual’s criminal record, which may include:

  * **Arrests**
  * **Convictions**
  * **Sentences**
  * **Pending criminal charges**
  * **Court records**

In Washington State, a criminal background check can be conducted through several databases, including the **Washington State Patrol \(WSP\)** and **county court systems**. These checks are critical for organizations seeking to ensure the safety of their employees, tenants, and other stakeholders.

#### **Types of Criminal Background Checks Available in Washington State**

![Types of Criminal Background Checks Available in Washington State](https://precisehire.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Types-of-Criminal-Background-Checks-Available-in-Washington-State.png)

In Washington State, there are different types of [background checks](<https://www.mycriminalrecords.com/>) based on the depth of the investigation and the methods used:

  1. **Statewide Background Checks** : Conducted by the **Washington State Patrol \(WSP\)** , these checks access records from across the state. They can be used for general purposes, including employment and tenant screening.
  2. **Fingerprint-Based Checks** : These are the most thorough checks, as they use an individual’s fingerprints to search criminal databases. This method is used by employers, government agencies, and law enforcement.
  3. **National Background Checks** : These checks go beyond Washington State and pull criminal records from across the U.S. They are commonly used for individuals applying for jobs that involve interstate or national responsibilities.
  4. **Local or County Checks** : These checks are typically limited to a specific geographic area \(e.g., a particular county in Washington\). While they may be less comprehensive than state or national checks, they can still provide useful information about a person’s criminal history within a particular jurisdiction.

While criminal background checks often require payment for comprehensive reports, there are **free resources available** to obtain basic information. In addition to the **WSP WATCH** system, there are several other free resources to check criminal records:

  * **Court records** : Many Washington counties provide online access to their court case records, where individuals can search for public records regarding criminal charges, court dates, and sentencing information.
  * **Public databases** : Some free databases may aggregate criminal records for individuals who have had legal issues in Washington State. While they may not offer the same depth of information as paid services, they can still be helpful for basic checks.

It’s important to note that these **free checks** often have limitations. They may not provide the most up-to-date or comprehensive information, and the accuracy can vary depending on the source. For individuals requiring a more **detailed or official background check** , paid services like those provided by the **Washington State Patrol** or third-party companies might be necessary.

### **Legal Aspects of Criminal Background Checks in Washington State**

![Legal Aspects of Criminal Background Checks in Washington State](https://precisehire.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Legal-Aspects-of-Criminal-Background-Checks-in-Washington-State.png)

In Washington State, criminal background checks are governed by both **federal and state laws**. It’s essential to understand these laws to ensure compliance and avoid potential legal issues.

##### **1\. Fair Credit Reporting Act \(FCRA\)**

The **Fair Credit Reporting Act \(FCRA\)** is a **federal law** that regulates how background checks can be used, especially in employment situations. It requires employers to:

  * Obtain written consent from the individual before conducting a background check.
  * Provide a **notice** to the individual if the employer decides not to hire them based on the results of the background check.
  * Offer the individual the opportunity to **dispute any incorrect information** found in the report.

For employers using background checks for hiring decisions, compliance with the FCRA is crucial to avoid lawsuits or fines.

##### **2\. Washington State’s “Ban-the-Box” Law**

Washington State has implemented a **“ ban-the-box” law**, which prevents employers from asking about criminal history on job applications. This law is designed to give individuals with criminal records a fair opportunity to be considered for employment without being immediately disqualified due to their background.

However, employers are still allowed to conduct background checks later in the hiring process. **Criminal history** can only be considered after an **initial interview** or when a job offer has been extended.

##### **3\. Expungement and Sealing of Criminal Records**

In Washington State, certain criminal records can be **expunged** \(sealed or removed\) under specific conditions. If a record is expunged, it will not appear in background checks, giving individuals a fresh start. However, not all offenses are eligible for expungement, and it depends on the nature of the crime, the time that has passed, and whether the individual has met specific criteria.

For those seeking to **seal** their criminal records, it is important to seek **legal advice** to determine eligibility.
