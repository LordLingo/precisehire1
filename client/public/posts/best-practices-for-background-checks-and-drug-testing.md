### Background Checks and Employee Drug Tests: What’s the Difference?

**Background checks** typically serve to verify a candidate’s history, including criminal records, academic qualifications, employment history, and references. The primary goal of conducting these checks is to assess a candidate’s past behavior and gauge their suitability for the role. Thorough background checks help mitigate risks associated with fraud, theft, or workplace violence.

On the other hand, **employee drug tests** are designed to detect recent use of substances such as alcohol, illegal drugs, or certain prescription medications. The intent here is to ensure a safe, healthy, and productive workplace, particularly in environments where safety is paramount, such as transportation, healthcare, and law enforcement.

### The Purpose and Scope of Each Screening Method

Professionals in the HR realm must recognize the significance of both screening methods in establishing a trustworthy workforce. According to [the source](<https://www.backgroundchecks.com/drug-screening/what-comes-first-the-background-check-or-the-drug-test>), employers often conduct background checks first. This is generally seen as part of the due diligence process after a conditional job offer; companies may require both checks to be passed before finalizing hiring decisions.

In regulated sectors, such as transportation or healthcare, both screenings are often mandated by law. It’s crucial for HR professionals to stay informed about the specific requirements in their industry. For example, the [Equal Employment Opportunity Commission \(EEOC\)](<https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/background-checks-what-employers-need-know>) has outlined essential guidelines for implementing background checks to avoid discriminatory practices.

### Implementation Sequence: Timing Matters

A common practice is to conduct **background checks first or simultaneously** with drug tests. As previously mentioned, many companies make passing these tests a condition of employment after an offer has been extended. However, many jurisdictions have laws, such as “ban the box,” that necessitate delaying these checks until after a conditional offer has been made \([Background Checks](<https://www.backgroundchecks.com/drug-screening/what-comes-first-the-background-check-or-the-drug-test>)\).

Understanding local laws and organizational policies can clarify when and how to best sequence these checks. Consider these policy implications when implementing your screening processes:

  1. **Evaluate risks associated with the role** : For positions that involve higher risk—such as those requiring the operation of heavy machinery—immediate testing may be prudent.
  2. **Stay compliant with regulations** : This includes both federal and state legal requirements on drug testing and background checks.
  3. **Communicate clearly** : Transparency with candidates regarding what their checks will include will create a trust-based environment.

### Policy Considerations: Why Both Checks Matter

Conducting both background checks and drug tests can drastically reduce the organizations’ risk of hiring candidates who might pose a safety or reputational risk. According to sources \([GoodHire](<https://www.goodhire.com/screening/drug-testing/>) and [Veremark](<https://www.veremark.com/blog/background-checks-and-drug-screening-a-guide-for-employers>)\), drug testing is particularly impactful, as it has been shown to lower workplace accidents, absenteeism, and employee turnover rates.

Be mindful that privacy and employees’ rights must be respected throughout the screening process. This includes:

  * Providing notice about the checks being conducted.
  * Obtaining explicit consent from candidates.

### FAQ

#### What is the main purpose of background checks?

The main purpose of background checks is to assess a candidate’s past behavior and suitability for a role by verifying their history, including criminal records, employment history, and academic qualifications.

#### Why are drug tests important in the workplace?

Drug tests are important in the workplace to ensure a safe and healthy environment, particularly in safety-sensitive positions. They help mitigate risks associated with impairment due to substance use.

#### When should background checks be conducted?

Background checks are typically conducted post-application or after a conditional job offer has been made, although regulations may vary by jurisdiction.

#### What are the legal requirements for drug testing?

Legal requirements for drug testing can vary depending on the industry and jurisdiction, with some sectors requiring drug testing as part of compliance regulations.

#### How can automation help in screening processes?

Automation can streamline background check and drug testing processes by reducing manual errors, expediting procedures, and ensuring compliance, leading to a seamless experience for candidates.
