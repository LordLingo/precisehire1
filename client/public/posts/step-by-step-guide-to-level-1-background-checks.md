## **Introduction to Level 1 Background Check and Its Purpose**

A **Level 1 background check** is a basic screening process that is often used by employers during the hiring process to verify a candidate’s identity, criminal history, and employment credentials. This type of background check is generally used for roles that do not involve high levels of risk or security concerns.

* * *

#### **How is it Different from Level 2 or Level 3 Background Checks?**

A Level 1 background check is typically less comprehensive than a **Level 2 background check** , which involves fingerprinting and searches for more serious criminal activity, or a **Level 3 background check** , which includes a deeper investigation into a candidate’s history, including financial records and references.

* * *

#### **Key Components of a Level 1 Background Check**

![Key Components of a Level 1 Background Check](https://precisehire.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Key-Components-of-a-Level-1-Background-Check-visual-selection.png)

A [Level 1 background check](<https://www.mycriminalrecords.com/>) usually consists of the following checks:

  1. **Criminal History Check**
     * Searches county or state records to identify any misdemeanors or minor offenses.
     * Does not typically cover federal-level crimes or international records.
  2. **Employment Verification**
     * Confirms past employment, including job titles and dates of employment, to ensure that the candidate’s resume aligns with their actual work history.
  3. **Education Verification**
     * Validates the educational qualifications provided by the candidate. This includes checking degrees, certifications, and institutions attended.
  4. **Identity Verification**
     * Confirms the candidate’s identity using government-issued IDs or Social Security number \(SSN\) validation.
  5. **Credit History Check \(Optional\)**
     * May be included for certain roles, particularly those involving financial responsibilities.

* * *

#### **Why Employers Use Level 1 Background Checks**

Employers use [Level 1 background checks](<https://precisehire.com/background-checks-in-apartment-rentals/>) for several reasons, including:

  * **Risk Mitigation** : Ensuring that candidates do not have a criminal history that could pose a risk to the organization.
  * **Qualification Verification** : Confirming that the candidate’s claims regarding their qualifications and experience are accurate.
  * **Workplace Safety** : Creating a safer work environment by screening for potential red flags.
  * **Trustworthiness** : Building a team of employees who meet ethical and professional standards.

* * *

#### **Who Needs a Level 1 Background Check?**

Certain industries and roles typically require Level 1 background checks, including:

  * **Retail and Customer Service** : To ensure employees interact professionally with customers.
  * **Administrative Roles** : To verify qualifications and trustworthiness in handling sensitive information.
  * **Entry-Level Positions** : Often used for jobs that do not involve high-risk responsibilities but still require a basic level of screening.

#### **How a Level 1 Background Check is Conducted**

![How a Level 1 Background Check is Conducted](https://precisehire.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/How-a-Level-1-Background-Check-is-Conducted-visual-selection.png)

The process of conducting a **Level 1 background check** involves several key steps that ensure accuracy and compliance. Below is a detailed outline of the steps involved:

  1. **Obtaining Candidate Consent**
     * Before initiating a background check, employers must obtain written consent from the candidate, as required by the Fair Credit Reporting Act \(FCRA\).
  2. **Collecting Relevant Information**
     * Candidates provide personal details, such as full name, Social Security number \(SSN\), and past employment or educational records, which are used to initiate the background check.
  3. **Conducting the Checks**
     * **Identity Verification** : Validates the candidate’s identity using databases or government-issued IDs.
     * **Criminal History Check** : Searches local and state criminal databases to identify any past misdemeanors or minor offenses.
     * **Employment and Education Verification** : Cross-references the candidate’s resume with information from previous employers and educational institutions.
  4. **Compiling the Results**
     * Once the checks are completed, the data is compiled into a report that the employer reviews to make informed hiring decisions.
  5. **Communicating Results**
     * If the results reveal any discrepancies or issues, employers are advised to discuss them with the candidate before making a final decision.

* * *

#### **Typical Timeline for a Level 1 Background Check**

A Level 1 background check is known for its quick turnaround time. On average, the process takes:

**Type of Check** | **Estimated Time**  
---|---  
Identity Verification | Instant \(or up to 1 day\)  
Criminal History Check | 1–3 business days  
Employment Verification | 2–5 business days  
Education Verification | 2–5 business days  
  
Delays can occur if information is incomplete, difficult to verify, or if third parties \(e.g., past employers\) are slow to respond.

#### **What Employers Should Expect in Terms of Results**

Employers can expect the following outcomes from a Level 1 background check:

  * **Clean Record** : Indicates no red flags in the candidate’s criminal or employment history.
  * **Discrepancies** : May show inconsistencies between the candidate’s resume and verified information.
  * **Red Flags** : May include minor criminal offenses, gaps in employment history, or falsified qualifications.

Employers should interpret results carefully and consider the relevance of any findings to the specific role.

#### **Considerations for Employers When Using Level 1 Background Checks**

While Level 1 background checks are beneficial, employers should also be aware of certain limitations and considerations:

  1. **Limited Scope**
     * Level 1 checks focus on basic information and may not uncover serious criminal offenses or federal-level crimes.
  2. **Potential for Inaccuracies**
     * Errors in public records or mismatches in identity can lead to incorrect findings.
  3. **Legal Compliance**
     * Employers must comply with FCRA regulations, state laws, and Equal Employment Opportunity \(EEO\) guidelines.
  4. **Not Suitable for High-Risk Roles**
     * For positions involving sensitive data, access to vulnerable populations, or high security, a more comprehensive background check \(e.g., Level 2 or Level 3\) may be necessary.

#### **Legal Aspects of Level 1 Background Checks**

![Legal Aspects of Level 1 Background Checks](https://precisehire.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Legal-Aspects-of-Level-1-Background-Checks-visual-selection.png)

When conducting a **Level 1 background check** , employers must adhere to a range of legal requirements designed to protect the rights of candidates and ensure fair hiring practices. Understanding these regulations is critical to avoiding legal issues and maintaining compliance.

* * *

**1\. The Fair Credit Reporting Act \(FCRA\)**  
The FCRA governs the use of consumer reports, including background checks, in employment decisions. Key requirements include:

  * **Candidate Consent** : Employers must obtain written consent from candidates before conducting a background check.
  * **Disclosure** : Employers must provide a clear and standalone disclosure form explaining that a background check will be performed.
  * **Adverse Action Process** : If an employer decides not to hire a candidate based on background check results, they must: 
    * Notify the candidate with a **pre-adverse action letter** and provide a copy of the report.
    * Allow the candidate a reasonable time to dispute or clarify the findings.
    * Send a final **adverse action notice** if the decision to rescind the job offer is upheld.

* * *

**2\. Equal Employment Opportunity \(EEO\) Laws**  
The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission \(EEOC\) enforces federal laws prohibiting discrimination during the hiring process. Employers must ensure that background checks:

  * Are applied consistently to all candidates regardless of race, gender, age, or other protected characteristics.
  * Do not result in **disparate impact** , where a neutral policy disproportionately excludes members of a protected group.
  * Focus only on offenses or findings relevant to the role \(e.g., recent criminal activity related to job responsibilities\).

* * *

**3\. State-Specific Laws and Ban-the-Box Legislation**  
Many states and local jurisdictions have implemented **ban-the-box** laws, which prohibit employers from asking about criminal history on initial job applications. Key points include:

  * Employers can only inquire about criminal history after making a conditional job offer.
  * Some states impose stricter limitations on what background check information can be used \(e.g., California prohibits reporting criminal convictions older than seven years\).

* * *

**4\. Privacy and Confidentiality**  
Employers must handle background check results with care to protect candidates’ privacy. This includes:

  * Securing sensitive information, such as Social Security numbers and financial data.
  * Limiting access to background check reports to authorized personnel only.
  * Destroying records after they are no longer needed, in compliance with retention policies.

* * *

#### **When Can Employers Use Level 1 Background Checks?**

Employers can use Level 1 background checks at various stages of the hiring process, provided they comply with legal requirements. Common scenarios include:

  * **Pre-Employment Screening** : To verify a candidate’s qualifications and check for potential red flags.
  * **Periodic Checks for Current Employees** : For roles that require ongoing verification, such as those involving financial responsibilities.
  * **Post-Conditional Offers** : To comply with state laws that restrict background checks until after a conditional job offer is made.

* * *

#### **What to Do If a Candidate Disputes a Level 1 Background Check Result**

Disputes over background check results can arise due to errors or misunderstandings. Employers should follow these steps to address disputes:

  1. **Provide a Copy of the Report**
     * Share the background check report with the candidate and identify the specific findings in question.
  2. **Re-Verify the Information**
     * Contact the background check provider to re-verify the disputed information. Providers are required by law to investigate disputes and correct errors within 30 days.
  3. **Allow the Candidate to Respond**
     * Give the candidate an opportunity to provide additional documentation or explanations, such as proof of expunged records or corrected employment dates.
  4. **Make a Final Decision**
     * If the dispute is resolved in the candidate’s favor, proceed with the hiring process. If not, provide a final adverse action notice and document the decision-making process.

#### **FAQs About Level 1 Background Checks**

###  __What’s the Difference Between a Level 1 and a Level 2 Background Check?

A Level 1 background check includes basic screenings, such as criminal history at the state or county level, employment verification, and identity checks. A Level 2 background check is more comprehensive, often including fingerprinting, FBI database searches, and checks for offenses involving vulnerable populations.

###  __How Long Does a Level 1 Background Check Take to Complete?

Level 1 background checks typically take 1–5 business days to complete, depending on the availability of information and responsiveness of third parties \(e.g., former employers or educational institutions\).

###  __What Types of Positions Require a Level 1 Background Check?

Level 1 background checks are commonly used for roles in retail, customer service, office administration, and other positions that do not involve high levels of risk or access to sensitive data.

###  __Do Level 1 Background Checks Include a Drug Test?

No, drug testing is not typically included in a Level 1 background check. Employers must request drug screening as a separate process.

###  __Can a Negative Level 1 Background Check Result Lead to a Job Offer Being Rescinded?

Yes, but only if the findings are relevant to the role and the employer follows proper legal procedures, including notifying the candidate and allowing them to dispute the results.
