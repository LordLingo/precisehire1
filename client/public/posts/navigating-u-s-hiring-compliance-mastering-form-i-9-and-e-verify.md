### Understanding Form I-9 and E-Verify

#### The Necessity of Form I-9

Form I-9 is a mandatory employment eligibility verification form that must be completed for all individuals hired for work in the United States after November 6, 1986. Understanding its requirements is crucial to avoid steep penalties that can arise from noncompliance, which can range from $288 to nearly $29,000 depending on the severity of the infraction \([Paycom](<https://www.paycom.com/resources/blog/form-i-9/>)\).

##### Key Requirements of Form I-9:

  1. **Identity and Work Authorization Verification:** Employers must physically examine original documents from new hires that verify identity and work authorization. Photocopies are generally not acceptable unless using an authorized agent or electronic system in a remote hiring context \([FADV](<https://fadv.com/solutions/form-i9-compliance/>)\).
  2. **Completion Deadline:** The form must be completed within three business days of a new hire’s start date.
  3. **Document Retention:** After completion, the I-9 forms must be retained for a specified period. This includes regularly reviewing Section 3 when an employee’s work authorization is time-limited \([UT Austin](<https://hr.utexas.edu/manager/hiring/form-i9-everify-resources>)\).

#### E-Verify: A Complementary Tool

E-Verify is a federal web-based system that allows employers to confirm the work eligibility of their employees by comparing the information provided in Form I-9 against records held by the Social Security Administration \(SSA\) and the Department of Homeland Security \(DHS\). While E-Verify is voluntary for most employers, it becomes mandatory for federal contractors and in specific states or jurisdictions \([E-Verify](<https://www.e-verify.gov/about-e-verify/what-is-e-verify/>)\).

##### E-Verify Specifics:

  1. **Data Requirements:** Unlike Form I-9, E-Verify requires the employee’s Social Security number and a photo ID for documentation.
  2. **Prompt Reporting:** Employers registered with E-Verify must submit their new hires’ data within three days of the employee’s start date.
  3. **Noncompliance Penalties:** Employers who fail to comply with E-Verify’s requirements may face fines ranging from $973 to $1,942 for each violation \([Paycom](<https://www.paycom.com/resources/blog/form-i-9/>)\).

### Best Practices for Document Verification

Given the complexities of compliance, adopting efficient and effective practices is vital. Here are several best practices for HR professionals and recruiters to ensure compliance with Form I-9 and E-Verify:

#### 1\. Internal Audits

Regular internal audits of completed I-9 forms can help identify errors before government inspections occur. This proactive approach not only ensures compliance but also helps maintain a clear record of hiring practices \([Paycom](<https://www.paycom.com/resources/blog/form-i-9/>)\).

#### 2\. Embrace Digital Solutions

With remote work on the rise, leveraging electronic I-9 management systems can streamline compliance processes. These solutions provide security, efficient retention, and easy access for audits, significantly reducing the administrative burden on HR teams \([FADV](<https://fadv.com/solutions/form-i9-compliance/>)\).

#### 3\. Continuous Training

Investing in training sessions and resources for HR professionals is crucial. The M-274 Handbook for Employers by USCIS provides authoritative guidance on completing I-9s. Additionally, many organizations offer online seminars that help HR professionals stay updated with compliance requirements \([E-Verify](<https://www.e-verify.gov/about-e-verify/what-is-e-verify/>)\).

#### 4\. Handle Rehires Appropriately

For rehired employees, Section 3 of Form I-9 can be used as long as it is within three years of the original hire date and current work authorization is verified. For longer breaks, a new I-9 form will need to be completed \([UT Austin](<https://hr.utexas.edu/manager/hiring/form-i9-everify-resources>)\).

### Consequences of Noncompliance

Understanding the penalties associated with noncompliance with Form I-9 and E-Verify is crucial in creating a culture of compliance within your organization. Some key consequences include:

  * **Paperwork Errors:** Fines can range from $288 to $2,861 per form for errors \([Paycom](<https://www.paycom.com/resources/blog/form-i-9/>)\).
  * **Employing Unauthorized Workers:** First offenses can lead to fines of between $716 and $5,724, escalating to $28,619 for repeat offenses \([Paycom](<https://www.paycom.com/resources/blog/form-i-9/>)\).
  * **E-Verify Noncompliance:** Failing to resolve a Final Nonconfirmation can incur fines ranging between $973 and $1,942 for each instance \([Paycom](<https://www.paycom.com/resources/blog/form-i-9/>)\).
