### **What is a Criminal Background Check?**

A criminal background check is a process in which information about an individual’s criminal history is obtained from various sources. These sources include public records, court records, and databases containing information about arrests, convictions, and incarcerations. In Oklahoma, this check can reveal details regarding misdemeanors, felonies, and other related criminal offenses that an individual may have been involved in.

Criminal background checks serve as an essential tool in helping employers, landlords, and legal authorities make informed decisions. For example, employers may want to verify whether potential employees have a history of violence, theft, or other serious offenses, while landlords may screen tenants to ensure they are not placing their property or other tenants at risk.

### **Types of Criminal Background Checks in Oklahoma**

Criminal background checks in Oklahoma come in different forms, with each serving different purposes and revealing varying degrees of information. Below are the main types of background checks conducted in Oklahoma:

#### **State Criminal Background Check**

The Oklahoma State Bureau of Investigation \(OSBI\) offers a state-level criminal background check that provides information on an individual’s criminal history within the state of Oklahoma. This type of check includes:

  * **Arrest records**
  * **Conviction information**
  * **Sex offender registry status**

It is one of the most commonly requested types of criminal background checks for employers, landlords, and other entities seeking to assess an individual’s history in Oklahoma.

#### **Federal Criminal Background Check**

A federal criminal background check focuses on criminal activity that falls under federal jurisdiction. This includes crimes such as immigration violations, drug trafficking, and federal tax evasion. These records are accessible through the FBI’s National Criminal Background Check System \(NCIC\). A federal background check is typically required for jobs related to federal agencies or positions involving high-security clearance.

#### **Local Criminal Background Check**

Local background checks provide criminal history records from county or municipal databases. These records may include arrests, citations, and convictions within a specific locality. Local checks are useful for obtaining information that may not appear in state or federal databases, particularly if the offense was committed at a local level.

#### **National Criminal Background Check**

A national criminal background check searches databases across the entire United States to gather information from different jurisdictions. This type of check is helpful for individuals who may have committed crimes in other states or those who may have relocated frequently.

### **Limitations of Criminal Background Checks in Oklahoma**

While criminal background checks provide valuable information, there are limitations to the data that can be accessed. It’s important to be aware of these limitations when conducting a background check:

  1. **Incomplete or Inaccurate Records**

Criminal records can sometimes be incomplete or contain inaccuracies. For example, an individual may be arrested but not convicted, yet their arrest record might still appear on a background check. Similarly, cases may be dismissed, but records may not be updated in a timely manner. These discrepancies can lead to false positives in background checks.

  2. **Expunged and Sealed Records**

In Oklahoma, certain criminal records can be sealed or expunged, meaning they are no longer accessible through most background checks. Expungement is typically available for individuals who have successfully completed their sentences and have no subsequent criminal activity. Individuals who have had their records expunged may still be able to claim that they do not have a criminal history, but this information will not appear in most background checks.

  3. **Jurisdictional Limitations**

While state background checks can provide information on criminal activity within Oklahoma, they may not include records from other states or federal offenses. This is why it is important to also conduct federal and national background checks when necessary.

  4. **Delayed Processing**

Processing delays can sometimes affect the accuracy and timeliness of criminal background checks. If records are not updated promptly or there is an issue with the provider, it can lead to inaccurate or incomplete results.

### **Legal Aspects of Criminal Background Checks in Oklahoma**

![Legal Aspects of Criminal Background Checks in Oklahoma](https://precisehire.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Legal-Aspects-of-Criminal-Background-Checks-in-Oklahoma.png)

Criminal background checks in Oklahoma are regulated by both state and federal laws to ensure that individuals’ rights are protected while balancing public safety and legal compliance. Below are the key legal considerations involved in criminal background checks:

#### **Fair Credit Reporting Act \(FCRA\)**

The Fair Credit Reporting Act \(FCRA\) is a federal law that governs the collection, dissemination, and use of consumer information, including criminal background checks. Under the FCRA, employers, landlords, and other entities must obtain written consent from an individual before conducting a criminal background check. The law also mandates that individuals be informed if they are denied employment or housing based on information in their background check, and they must be given the opportunity to dispute any inaccuracies.

The FCRA also places restrictions on how long certain criminal records can be reported. For example, arrests that did not result in convictions cannot typically be reported after seven years, while convictions can be reported indefinitely for employment purposes unless they are expunged.

#### **Equal Employment Opportunity \(EEO\) Laws**

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission \(EEOC\) enforces federal laws that prohibit discrimination in hiring based on race, color, national origin, sex, disability, or religion. When using criminal background checks in hiring, employers must ensure that their policies are compliant with EEO laws.

The EEOC guidelines on criminal background checks recommend that employers assess whether the criminal record is directly related to the job in question. Employers must also consider factors such as the nature of the crime, how long ago it occurred, and whether the applicant has demonstrated rehabilitation.

#### **Oklahoma State Laws**

Oklahoma has specific laws that govern criminal background checks, especially regarding employment and housing decisions. Under Oklahoma law, individuals cannot be discriminated against solely based on a criminal record unless the offense is directly related to the job or tenancy. Employers and landlords must follow certain procedures to ensure that their use of criminal background checks is non-discriminatory.

Additionally, Oklahoma law allows for the expungement of certain criminal records under specific circumstances. Expungement is a legal process that removes an individual’s criminal record from public access, which means that expunged offenses do not appear in most background checks.

#### **Expungement and Sealing of Criminal Records in Oklahoma**

Expungement refers to the process by which an individual’s criminal record is legally erased from public view, making it inaccessible to most employers, landlords, and others conducting background checks. In Oklahoma, certain criminal offenses may be eligible for expungement, including those related to non-violent offenses, successful completion of a deferred sentence, or certain arrests that did not lead to convictions.

To expunge a criminal record in Oklahoma, individuals must file a petition with the court. If granted, the individual’s criminal record will be sealed, and it will no longer appear on criminal background checks conducted by employers or other entities. Expungement helps individuals with past convictions regain opportunities for employment and housing.

For individuals with a sealed or expunged record, Oklahoma law protects their right to deny having a criminal record on job applications and in other situations where the question is asked.

### **Frequently Asked Questions \(FAQs\)**

###  __How Long Does a Criminal Background Check Take in Oklahoma?

The processing time for a criminal background check in Oklahoma depends on the type of check being conducted. ###  __Can Someone Be Denied Housing Due to a Criminal Background Check in Oklahoma?

Yes, in some cases, a person can be denied housing based on their criminal background. ###  __Can I Get My Criminal Record Expunged in Oklahoma?

Yes, it is possible to have certain criminal records expunged in Oklahoma, provided the individual meets specific eligibility requirements. This typically includes non-violent offenses, certain arrests without convictions, and successful completion of a deferred sentence. To begin the expungement process, individuals must file a petition with the court, and the court will decide whether to grant the request.

###  __Do Employers in Oklahoma Have to Conduct Criminal Background Checks?

No, employers in Oklahoma are not required by law to conduct criminal background checks. However, many employers choose to do so to mitigate risk and ensure the safety and integrity of their workforce. Employers are required to obtain written consent from applicants before conducting a background check, and they must follow non-discriminatory practices as outlined by the EEOC and the FCRA.

###  __Are Criminal Background Checks Public Record in Oklahoma?

Certain criminal background information, such as arrest and conviction records, is part of the public record in Oklahoma. However, expunged or sealed records are not accessible to the public. Employers, landlords, and other entities can typically access public criminal records through background check services or by directly contacting the appropriate government agencies. Some records, such as those related to juvenile offenses, may be restricted from public view.
