### Introduction to Criminal Background Check Errors

A criminal background check is a process used by employers, landlords, and other organizations to assess an individual’s criminal history. These checks are conducted to ensure that a person does not have a history of criminal activity that could pose a risk to others or the organization itself. Criminal [background checks](<https://www.exactbackgroundchecks.com/>) can provide crucial insights into an individual’s past, including arrests, convictions, and sometimes even pending charges.

These background checks may be used for various reasons, such as:

  * **Employment Screening** : Employers often conduct criminal background checks as part of their hiring process to ensure the safety and security of the workplace. This helps employers make informed decisions about potential employees, especially for roles requiring trust, such as those involving children, the elderly, or financial responsibilities.
  * **Housing Applications** : Landlords and property management companies may require criminal background checks to determine if potential tenants have a history of criminal activity that could jeopardize the safety of other residents or the property.
  * **Loan and Financial Applications** : Financial institutions may perform background checks when individuals apply for loans or credit to assess their reliability.
  * **Licensing** : Certain industries require individuals to pass criminal background checks before granting professional licenses \(e.g., healthcare providers, lawyers, etc.\).

Criminal background checks can vary based on jurisdiction and the level of information needed. For example, federal background checks may reveal nationwide criminal records, while state or county checks typically only cover local offenses.

#### Common Errors Found on Criminal Background Checks

While criminal background checks are an essential tool for evaluating an individual’s history, errors in the information reported can have serious consequences. Some of the most common errors that appear on criminal background checks include:

  1. **Mistaken Identity** : One of the most frequent errors is the misidentification of an individual due to shared names, similar birthdates, or other matching personal details. For instance, a person named “John Smith” with a similar birthdate to someone with a criminal record may have their information wrongly linked to the criminal’s history.
  2. **Reporting Inaccuracies** : In some cases, criminal background checks may include incorrect charges, dates, or court records. For example, an individual might be mistakenly listed as having been convicted of a crime they were acquitted of, or their sentence may be reported inaccurately.
  3. **Outdated or Sealed Records** : Some background checks may incorrectly report outdated or sealed records. A criminal record that was expunged or sealed by the courts may still appear, affecting an individual’s ability to secure employment or housing.
  4. **Duplicate Records** : Errors can also occur if the same person’s record is reported multiple times due to clerical errors, causing confusion or unnecessary concern for those undergoing background checks.
  5. **Incorrect Address or Contact Information** : Sometimes, [background checks](<https://www.mycriminalrecords.com/>) include errors related to personal details, such as incorrect addresses or misspelled names. These discrepancies may cause delays or complications during the verification process.

#### How Do Errors on Criminal Background Checks Happen?

Errors in criminal background checks can occur at several stages of the process:

  1. **Human Error** : Background check companies may make clerical or typographical errors when inputting data into their systems. For example, misspelling a name or incorrectly entering a date of birth can lead to a mismatch or false report.
  2. **Data Entry Mistakes** : Criminal history data is often pulled from multiple sources, such as court records, police databases, and arrest records. If the information is entered incorrectly at any of these points, it can lead to inaccurate or incomplete reports.
  3. **Database Discrepancies** : Criminal background checks typically rely on large databases containing public records. Sometimes, the data stored in these databases is outdated or incorrect. If someone’s criminal record is expunged or sealed and the database is not updated, it may still show up on a background check.
  4. **Faulty or Outdated Records** : Records may not be updated in real time, especially if they pertain to older cases or jurisdictions with less efficient record-keeping systems. This can result in outdated information showing up on a background check.
  5. **Identity Theft or Fraud** : In some cases, an individual’s criminal history may be incorrectly attributed to another person due to identity theft. If someone uses another person’s identity for criminal activity, it can cause discrepancies on background checks.

#### Steps to Identify Errors in a Criminal Background Check

Before correcting any errors, it’s essential to identify them. Individuals should follow these steps to review their criminal background check:

  1. **Request a Copy of Your Criminal Background Check** : The first step is to obtain a copy of the criminal background check report. In most cases, individuals can request a free copy of their report, especially if the check is being used for employment purposes.
  2. **Review the Report Carefully** : Once you receive the report, review it thoroughly to check for discrepancies. Pay attention to personal information, such as your name, date of birth, and addresses, as well as any criminal charges or convictions listed.
  3. **Cross-Check with Your Records** : Compare the information on the background check with your own records. If you notice any differences, such as a charge that does not belong to you, this may indicate an error.
  4. **Look for Outdated or Expunged Records** : If you have any criminal records that were sealed or expunged, ensure that they are not listed on the report.
  5. **Contact the Reporting Agency** : If you find any discrepancies, contact the background check company or agency responsible for providing the report. Most agencies will provide you with instructions on how to dispute errors.

#### How to Dispute Errors on a Criminal Background Check

![How to Dispute Errors on a Criminal Background Check](https://precisehire.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/How-to-Dispute-Errors-on-a-Criminal-Background-Check.png)

When errors are identified on a criminal background check, it’s crucial to take the appropriate steps to correct them as quickly as possible. An inaccurate report can have severe consequences for employment, housing, and even legal matters. Below is a step-by-step guide on how to dispute and correct errors on a criminal background check.

  1. **Contact the Background Check Company or Agency**  
The first step is to reach out to the company or agency that conducted the background check. This could be a private background check agency or a government body that oversees criminal records. Most background check agencies allow individuals to dispute errors either by phone, email, or online portal.
  2. **Review the Error and Gather Proof**  
Once you’ve identified the error, you must gather the necessary documents to prove that the information on the background check is incorrect. Common documentation to support your case includes:

     * **Court documents** showing that the charges were dismissed, expunged, or not applicable.
     * **Identification documents** , such as a driver’s license or passport, to confirm your identity.
     * **Records from the police or jail** showing that you were not involved in the crime in question.
  3. **Submit a Dispute Form**  
Most agencies require you to fill out a dispute form. This form will ask for details regarding the error and the supporting documentation. Be as specific as possible when describing the discrepancy to ensure the agency understands what needs to be corrected.
  4. **Follow Up to Ensure Timely Resolution**  
After submitting your dispute, it’s important to follow up with the agency to confirm that they’ve received your documents and are processing the dispute. Keep records of all your communications and responses. The agency is generally required by law to resolve the dispute within a certain period—typically 30 to 45 days.
  5. **Get a Copy of the Updated Report**  
Once the error has been corrected, request a copy of your updated criminal background check to ensure that the changes have been made. Review the report carefully to ensure the accuracy of the corrections.

#### Legal Rights Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act \(FCRA\)

The **Fair Credit Reporting Act \(FCRA\)** provides legal protections for individuals when it comes to disputes over inaccuracies in background checks. If you discover an error on your criminal background check, the FCRA gives you the right to dispute the information and requires the reporting agency to investigate and correct any errors.

The FCRA mandates the following protections:

  * **Right to Dispute** : You have the right to dispute any information on your background check that you believe is inaccurate or incomplete. The reporting agency must investigate your dispute, typically within 30 days.
  * **Right to Access** : Under the FCRA, you are entitled to a free copy of your background check if it has been used against you in an adverse decision, such as a job rejection. You can request a copy of your report from the agency that conducted the background check.
  * **Obligation to Correct Errors** : If the agency determines that the information is incorrect, they must correct the error and notify you. They also must notify any parties who received the inaccurate report in the past.
  * **Right to Sue** : If a background check company fails to correct errors or violates the FCRA, you have the right to sue for damages.

#### Legal Aspects of Criminal Background Check Errors

![Legal Aspects of Criminal Background Check Errors](https://precisehire.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Legal-Aspects-of-Criminal-Background-Check-Errors.png)

When errors on a criminal background check are not addressed properly, they can lead to significant legal consequences. Understanding the legal framework surrounding these errors is crucial, as it ensures that individuals know their rights and what actions they can take to rectify the situation.

##### Relevant Laws and Regulations

Several laws and regulations protect individuals from being negatively impacted by errors in criminal background checks:

  1. **Fair Credit Reporting Act \(FCRA\)**  
The **Fair Credit Reporting Act \(FCRA\)** is the primary law governing how background checks are conducted and how errors must be handled. Under the FCRA:

     * Individuals have the right to dispute inaccurate information on their background checks.
     * Background check agencies must investigate disputes within 30 days.
     * If the dispute results in changes, agencies must notify the individual and any organizations that received the inaccurate report.
     * The FCRA requires companies to ensure the information provided in a background check is accurate and up-to-date.
  2. **Equal Employment Opportunity Commission \(EEOC\) Guidelines**  
The **EEOC** provides guidelines on how criminal background checks should be handled in employment decisions. Employers are prohibited from using criminal records as a blanket disqualification tool. Instead, employers must consider the nature of the crime, the time passed since the offense, and its relevance to the position in question. Additionally:

     * Employers are required to notify applicants if a criminal background check was used to make an adverse decision, such as denying a job.
     * Applicants must be given the opportunity to dispute any inaccurate information before a final decision is made.
  3. **State-Specific Laws**  
Various states have their own regulations regarding background checks, particularly when it comes to the use of criminal records in employment or housing decisions. For example, some states have “ban the box” laws, which prohibit employers from asking about criminal history on job applications. Additionally:

     * States like California and New York have laws that provide individuals with specific rights to dispute criminal record inaccuracies.
     * Some jurisdictions have more stringent rules about expunging or sealing criminal records, which can also affect how background checks are conducted.

##### Legal Implications of Background Check Errors

When errors on a criminal background check are not corrected or acknowledged, both individuals and employers may face legal challenges. For instance:

  * **Employment Discrimination** : If a person is wrongfully disqualified from a job due to an inaccurate criminal background check, it could lead to claims of discrimination. The EEOC mandates that employers consider more than just the existence of a criminal record in hiring decisions.
  * **Housing Discrimination** : If an error on a criminal background check prevents an individual from securing housing, they may have legal recourse under the Fair Housing Act, which protects individuals from discrimination based on their criminal history.
  * **Legal Liability for Employers** : Employers who fail to properly investigate or dispute errors on criminal background checks could face legal action. If an applicant is unjustly rejected due to inaccurate information, the employer may be held liable for damages under the FCRA.

#### Frequently Asked Questions \(FAQs\)

###  __How Long Does It Take to Correct an Error on a Criminal Background Check?

The correction process can take anywhere from 30 to 45 days, depending on the agency handling the dispute. In some cases, if the error involves legal documents or court records, it may take longer to resolve. It’s important to follow up regularly to ensure the process is moving forward.

###  __What Should I Do if I Discover an Error After a Job Offer Has Been Made?

If you discover an error after receiving a job offer, contact the employer immediately. Inform them of the mistake and provide supporting documentation to prove the error. The employer is required to give you a chance to correct the issue before making a final hiring decision.

###  __Can I Be Denied Employment or Housing Due to a Background Check Error?

Yes, errors on a criminal background check can lead to employment or housing denials. However, under the **FCRA** , you have the right to dispute inaccuracies, and the company conducting the background check must rectify the issue if it’s proven to be incorrect.

###  __How Do I Prove That an Error on My Criminal Background Check Is Not Mine?

You can prove that an error is not yours by submitting documentation that clearly identifies you and demonstrates that you were not involved in the criminal activity in question. This may include:

  1.      * Court documents showing charges were dismissed or expunged.
     * Proof of identity such as a passport or driver’s license.
     * A letter from the police or court confirming the error.

###  __What Happens if the Background Check Company Does Not Correct the Error?

If a background check company fails to correct an error, you may file a formal complaint with the **Consumer Financial Protection Bureau \(CFPB\)** or your state attorney general’s office. Under the **FCRA** , you also have the right to sue the background check company for damages caused by their failure to correct the inaccurate information.
