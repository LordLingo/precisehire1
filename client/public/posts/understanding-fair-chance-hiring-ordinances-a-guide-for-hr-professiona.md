### What Are Fair Chance Hiring Ordinances?

Fair Chance Hiring Ordinances, often referred to as [“Ban the Box”](<https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/32/2022/11/Fair-Chance-Act-FAQ_ENG.pdf>) laws, are regulations that require employers to delay inquiries into an applicant’s criminal history until after a conditional job offer has been made. These laws aim to reduce hiring barriers for individuals with criminal records, providing them equitable access to employment opportunities without the prejudice that often accompanies past convictions.

### Scope of Coverage

While the specifics of Fair Chance Hiring Ordinances vary by location, they generally apply to employers with a minimum number of employees. For example, California’s Fair Chance Act encompasses businesses with five or more employees [\(source\)](<https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/32/2022/11/Fair-Chance-Act-FAQ_ENG.pdf>). Furthermore, the coverage extends beyond full-time employees to include part-time workers, temporary hires, and seasonal staff.

### Core Requirements of Fair Chance Hiring

Employers must adhere to several core requirements when it comes to Fair Chance Hiring:

  1. **Delay Background Checks** : Employers cannot inquire about or consider an applicant’s criminal history until after a conditional job offer is made [\(source\)](<https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/32/2022/11/Fair-Chance-Act-FAQ_ENG.pdf>).
  2. **Ban the Box** : Job applications must not include questions about criminal records, effectively “banning the box” that many applicants would commonly check [\(source\)](<https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/32/2022/11/Fair-Chance-Act-FAQ_ENG.pdf>).
  3. **Individual Assessment Required** : Some ordinances mandate an individualized assessment and require written documentation before rescinding job offers based on criminal history [\(source\)](<https://www.gtlaw-laborandemployment.com/2023/09/background-check-it-out-understanding-californias-new-fair-chance-act-regulations-on-criminal-records-and-background-checks-when-making-employment-decisions/>).
  4. **Exclusions** : Certain types of criminal history, like arrests not resulting in convictions or records that have been expunged, are not to be considered in the hiring process [\(source\)](<https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/32/2022/11/Fair-Chance-Act-FAQ_ENG.pdf>).

### State vs. Local Variations

Fair Chance Hiring Ordinances can differ significantly between state and local jurisdictions. For instance, California established its Fair Chance Act, effective January 1, 2018, which sets statewide requirements for background checks [\(source\)](<https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/32/2022/11/Fair-Chance-Act-FAQ_ENG.pdf>). In many cases, municipalities have a role in enacting stricter or supplementary ordinances, like the Los Angeles County Fair Chance Ordinance effective in 2024, which designates local enforcement to the County Department of Consumer and Business Affairs [\(source\)](<https://opportunity.lacounty.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/FCO-FAQ-Final-Updated-with-Recommendations.pdf>).

### Practical Impact on Employers

Statistics show that at least 37 states and 150 cities/counties have some form of Fair Chance or Ban-the-Box law, with the enforcement and requirements expanding annually [\(source\)](<https://iprospectcheck.com/fair-chance-hiring/>). Compliance with these ordinances is not just a matter of good practice; non-compliance can result in penalties, lawsuits, or negative publicity, making it crucial for organizations to keep their hiring policies up-to-date.

### Compliance and Adaptation Strategies

For organizations navigating the new landscape of Fair Chance Hiring, there are several practical steps that can be taken to maintain compliance and implement effective hiring practices:

  1. **Evaluate Applicable Laws** : Determine which Fair Chance Hiring ordinances apply based on your business’s location and size [\(source\)](<https://www.gtlaw-laborandemployment.com/2023/09/background-check-it-out-understanding-californias-new-fair-chance-act-regulations-on-criminal-records-and-background-checks-when-making-employment-decisions/>).
  2. **Revise Application Forms** : Remove all questions regarding criminal history from initial job applications. This change not only fosters compliance but also shifts focus towards applicants’ skills and qualifications.
  3. **Train HR Staff** : Provide training for HR professionals and hiring managers about the timing and nature of background checks and conducting individualized assessments before an applicant can be screened out based on criminal history [\(source\)](<https://www.gtlaw-laborandemployment.com/2023/09/background-check-it-out-understanding-californias-new-fair-chance-act-regulations-on-criminal-records-and-background-checks-when-making-employment-decisions/>).
  4. **Document Compliance Efforts** : Maintain detailed documentation of all compliance efforts, especially if local ordinances demand transparency or reporting. Proper recordkeeping can defend against allegations of non-compliance.
  5. **Stay Updated** : Regularly monitor updates in both state and local regulations, as new laws can be enacted or existing ones revised frequently [\(source\)](<https://iprospectcheck.com/fair-chance-hiring/>).

### FAQ

**Q: What does “Ban the Box” mean?**  
A: It refers to laws that prohibit employers from asking about criminal histories on job applications, encouraging a fair assessment based on qualifications.

**Q: Are all employers required to follow Fair Chance Hiring Ordinances?**  
A: Not all, as these laws typically apply to employers with a certain number of employees.

**Q: Can employers still run background checks?**  
A: Yes, but they can only do so after making a conditional job offer.

**Q: What happens if an employer violates these ordinances?**  
A: Violations can lead to penalties, lawsuits, or negative publicity.

**Q: How can HR departments stay informed about changes in legislation?**  
A: Regularly monitoring updates and participating in training can help HR departments stay compliant.
