### Understanding Candidates’ Rights in Background Checks

Candidates today are empowered like never before when it comes to their personal data. The legal frameworks and rights available to them must be understood by employers as they initiate background screening processes.

#### 1\. Consent and Notification

Under federal law like the **Fair Credit Reporting Act \(FCRA\)** , organizations must obtain a candidate’s written consent before conducting most background checks. This legal requirement ensures transparency and allows candidates to be aware of how their personal data is being utilized. In instances where a background check leads to an adverse hiring decision, candidates have the right to be informed and can review any inaccuracies in their reports [\(Veremark\)](<https://www.veremark.com/blog/10-tips-for-better-employee-background-checks>).

#### 2\. Access, Correction, and Deletion

Recent state privacy laws enacted in places like Oregon, Texas, and Florida provide individuals with even more power regarding their information. Beginning in July 2024, these laws will allow candidates to access, correct, and, in specific situations, delete personal data collected during background checks [\(BackgroundChecks.com\)](<https://www.backgroundchecks.com/compliance-and-legislation/new-privacy-laws-for-background-checks-in-several-states>). Such provisions empower candidates to ensure that they are represented accurately within hiring decisions.

#### 3\. Protection Against Discrimination

Candidates are also protected from discrimination based on information contained in background checks. Various regulations, like New York’s Article 23-A and “Ban the Box” laws in states such as California and Washington, limit when and how employers can inquire about criminal history, aiming to mitigate discrimination against marginalized groups [\(S2Verify\)](<https://s2verify.com/resource/background-screening-state-privacy-laws/>).

#### 4\. Expungement Protections

Some regions, including Michigan, implement measures for automatically sealing certain criminal records. This ensures that older or minor offenses do not impede candidates’ employment opportunities, fostering a more equitable hiring landscape [\(S2Verify\)](<https://s2verify.com/resource/background-screening-state-privacy-laws/>).

### Employer Obligations in Background Check Processes

While candidates are entitled to various rights, employers bear significant responsibilities in conducting background checks. Compliance with these obligations is not just about legal adherence; it can also enhance a company’s reputation and streamline its hiring processes.

#### 1\. Transparency

Clear communication is vital. Employers are now mandated to disclose what information they collect and the motivations behind it. State privacy laws emphasize limiting data collection to what is relevant for the job and providing candidates with detailed information about the data gathering process [\(BackgroundChecks.com\)](<https://www.backgroundchecks.com/compliance-and-legislation/new-privacy-laws-for-background-checks-in-several-states>).

#### 2\. Compliance with Laws

Employers must navigate complex legal requirements that vary based on jurisdiction. For example, while the FCRA sets national standards for consent and adverse action procedures, states like California impose stricter regulations on how employment background information may be used [\(S2Verify\)](<https://s2verify.com/resource/background-screening-state-privacy-laws/>). A thorough understanding of these variances is essential for national employers.

#### 3\. Data Handling and Security

Given the sensitive nature of the data involved, organizations are responsible for safeguarding personal data against unauthorized access and misuse. This includes ensuring that only key decision-makers access sensitive information. Failure to properly handle candidate data may lead to penalties and legal repercussions [\(BackgroundChecks.com\)](<https://www.backgroundchecks.com/compliance-and-legislation/new-privacy-laws-for-background-checks-in-several-states>).

#### 4\. Limitations on Data Use and Retention

Employers should also establish policies to avoid over-collecting or unnecessarily retaining personal information, as mandated by various privacy laws [\(BackgroundChecks.com\)](<https://www.backgroundchecks.com/compliance-and-legislation/new-privacy-laws-for-background-checks-in-several-states>).

### State Law Nuances Impacting Background Checks

Each state has unique regulations and norms around background checks that can complicate hiring processes:

  * **California:** Employers must not inquire about criminal history until a job offer has been made, and they are subject to strict privacy protections [\(S2Verify\)](<https://s2verify.com/resource/background-screening-state-privacy-laws/>).
  * **Michigan:** The state is progressing towards auto-sealing certain criminal records, further enhancing candidate privacy [\(S2Verify\)](<https://s2verify.com/resource/background-screening-state-privacy-laws/>).
  * **Oregon, Texas, Florida:** New privacy laws set to take effect in 2024 will impose restrictions on data collection and empower candidates with more rights over their personal information [\(BackgroundChecks.com\)](<https://www.backgroundchecks.com/compliance-and-legislation/new-privacy-laws-for-background-checks-in-several-states>).
  * **New York, New Jersey, Washington:** These states implement additional regulations that may limit inquiries about criminal history on initial job applications and provide candidates with specific remedies [\(S2Verify\)](<https://s2verify.com/resource/background-screening-state-privacy-laws/>).

### FAQ

**What should employers do if they find discrepancies in a candidate ’s background check?**

Employers should inform the candidate of the discrepancy and allow them an opportunity to contest or correct the information.

**How can candidates protect their rights during a background check?**

Candidates should be proactive in understanding their rights, request copies of their background checks, and correct any inaccuracies they may find.

**Are there penalties for non-compliance with background check laws?**

Yes, non-compliance can lead to severe legal consequences, including fines and damage to the company’s reputation.
