### What is a Criminal Background Check in Virginia?

A **criminal background check** in Virginia is a process used to verify an individual’s criminal history by accessing relevant records from state, local, and federal agencies. It helps determine if a person has been convicted of any crimes, including felonies, misdemeanors, or infractions. This check is typically used by employers, landlords, and other organizations to ensure safety and security.

There are several types of criminal background checks that may be conducted in Virginia:

  * **Statewide Criminal History Checks** : This is the most common type of background check, and it typically involves searching the **Virginia State Police** database. It includes information about arrests, convictions, and other legal proceedings within the state.
  * **Federal Background Checks** : These checks involve searching federal databases, such as the **FBI** records. They provide a broader look at an individual’s criminal history that may extend beyond the state level.
  * **Local Checks** : These are conducted at the county or city level and can include information from local police departments or courts. They may uncover additional details not available in statewide or federal databases.

These checks are vital in assessing whether an individual poses a risk to an organization or community. They provide a full picture of a person’s criminal background, ensuring the safety and compliance of those involved in high-trust environments.

### How Does the Process of Criminal Background Check Work in Virginia?

The process of obtaining a criminal background check in Virginia typically involves several steps, which can be initiated by individuals, employers, or other authorized entities. Here’s a step-by-step overview of how the process works:

  1. **Requesting the Check** : 
     * Individuals or employers can request a criminal background check through various channels, such as the **Virginia State Police** or the **FBI**.
     * **State Police** : Requests can be made online or by submitting a fingerprint card. Individuals will need to provide personal information such as their full name, date of birth, and address.
     * **FBI Background Check** : This requires fingerprinting, and the check is processed through federal databases.
     * **Online Services** : Many third-party providers allow individuals or businesses to request background checks through online platforms for quicker results.
  2. **Providing Information and Documents** : To initiate a background check, the person requesting the check will need to provide specific details, such as a valid form of identification \(e.g., driver’s license, passport\), as well as consent for the background check to be conducted. For fingerprint-based checks, the applicant will need to visit a designated fingerprinting agency.
  3. **Processing the Request** : Once the background check request is submitted, the agency will process the information. In the case of the **Virginia State Police** , the results typically take a few days to a few weeks, depending on the type of check requested and the method used.
  4. **Receiving the Results** : After the check is processed, the requester will receive a report detailing the individual’s criminal history. This report will include information about arrests, convictions, and any other relevant legal issues within the state. Federal checks will include data from the FBI’s national criminal database.
  5. **Using the Results** : Employers and landlords must handle the results of criminal background checks carefully. Virginia law requires that businesses adhere to regulations when using criminal history information in decision-making processes. For example, employers must ensure that any decisions based on a background check are consistent with the **Fair Credit Reporting Act \(FCRA\)** and cannot be discriminatory.

### Using Criminal Background Checks for Employment in Virginia

Criminal [background checks](<https://www.mycriminalrecords.com/>) are a crucial tool in the hiring process for many businesses in Virginia. They help employers assess whether a candidate is fit for the job, especially in sensitive roles that require trustworthiness and integrity. However, employers must comply with specific regulations when using criminal background check results for employment decisions.

#### **Fair Credit Reporting Act \(FCRA\) Compliance**

The **Fair Credit Reporting Act \(FCRA\)** is a federal law that governs how employers can use background checks for hiring purposes. It sets rules about the collection, use, and reporting of background check information. Employers in Virginia must follow these key provisions:

  * **Disclosure and Consent** : Employers must inform applicants that a background check will be conducted and obtain written consent before proceeding.
  * **Adverse Action** : If an employer decides not to hire an individual based on criminal history, they must provide a pre-adverse action notice, giving the candidate a chance to dispute the information. After that, if the employer still decides to reject the candidate, they must provide a final adverse action notice.
  * **Accuracy** : Employers must ensure that the information they use is accurate and up to date. Using incorrect or outdated data can lead to legal challenges.

#### **Criminal History in Hiring Decisions**

While criminal background checks are valuable tools in the hiring process, employers must be careful not to make decisions based solely on criminal history. In Virginia, the **Ban the Box** law prohibits employers from asking about criminal history on job applications. Employers are only allowed to inquire about criminal history once a conditional job offer has been made.

Employers should also consider the nature of the crime and how it relates to the job responsibilities. For instance, a conviction for a financial crime may be more relevant to a role in banking than to a job in a warehouse setting. Virginia law encourages employers to conduct an individualized assessment of an applicant’s criminal history and to avoid blanket policies that exclude candidates based solely on past convictions.

### Legal and Regulatory Aspects of Criminal Background Checks in Virginia

![Legal and Regulatory Aspects of Criminal Background Checks in Virginia](https://precisehire.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Legal-and-Regulatory-Aspects-of-Criminal-Background-Checks-in-Virginia.png)

Understanding the legal framework surrounding criminal background checks in Virginia is critical for both individuals and businesses. The use of criminal history data is regulated by both federal and state laws, and non-compliance with these regulations can lead to legal consequences. Below are the key legal considerations when conducting criminal background checks in Virginia:

#### **Federal and State Regulations**

  1. **Fair Credit Reporting Act \(FCRA\)** : The **FCRA** governs the use of background checks for employment purposes across the United States. It ensures that employers: 
     * Obtain written consent from the applicant before conducting a background check.
     * Provide a copy of the report if adverse action \(such as rejecting a job application\) is taken based on the findings.
     * Follow an adverse action process, which includes informing the applicant about the decision and offering a chance to dispute inaccuracies.
  2. **Virginia State Laws** : Virginia has several laws that regulate the use of criminal background information. The most important include: 
     * **Ban the Box Law** : Virginia prohibits employers from asking about criminal history on initial job applications. Employers can only inquire about criminal convictions after extending a conditional offer of employment.
     * **Virginia Human Rights Act** : This law prohibits discrimination in employment based on criminal history unless the crime is directly related to the job in question. Employers must consider the nature of the offense and how long ago it occurred before making a hiring decision.
     * **Expungement of Criminal Records** : Certain criminal convictions may be eligible for expungement under Virginia law, meaning they are removed from public record. Expungement can affect how background checks report an individual’s criminal history.
  3. **Federal Compliance \(EEOC Guidelines\)** : The **Equal Employment Opportunity Commission \(EEOC\)** provides guidelines on how criminal history should be used in employment decisions to avoid discrimination based on race, national origin, or other protected categories. Employers must ensure that their background check policies are consistent and applied fairly to all applicants.
  4. **Know Your Rights** : Individuals undergoing background checks in Virginia have certain rights under the FCRA. For example, if an employer takes adverse action based on a criminal background check, they must: 
     * Provide the individual with a copy of the report and allow them to dispute inaccuracies.
     * Inform the individual of their right to receive a copy of the report before any adverse action is finalized.

### Common Legal Issues in Criminal Background Checks

While criminal background checks are essential for protecting employers, landlords, and the public, they can also lead to legal challenges if mishandled. Some common legal issues include:

  1. **Inaccurate or Incomplete Criminal Records** : Mistakes in criminal records, such as outdated or incorrect information, can cause significant harm to individuals and create legal liability for the employer or agency using the background check. Individuals have the right to dispute errors in their criminal record, and employers must investigate any inaccuracies.
  2. **Failure to Obtain Consent** : Failing to obtain written consent from an applicant before conducting a criminal background check can violate the FCRA. Employers must ensure they are in compliance with this requirement to avoid potential legal consequences.
  3. **Discrimination Risks** : If an employer rejects a candidate based solely on a criminal conviction without considering the job’s requirements or the candidate’s rehabilitation, they may be at risk of discrimination lawsuits. It’s important for employers to assess the nature of the offense and its relevance to the job before making a decision.
  4. **Inadequate Adverse Action Procedures** : Employers must follow the proper procedures outlined by the FCRA if they decide to take adverse action \(e.g., denying employment\) based on a criminal background check. Failure to provide the proper notifications can result in legal penalties and lawsuits.

If you find errors in your background check, you should contact the agency that conducted the check and provide documentation to correct the mistake. Under the FCRA, you have the right to dispute inaccurate information, and the agency must investigate the issue.

In Virginia, criminal records are generally public, though certain records may be sealed or expunged under specific circumstances. Expunged records are not accessible to the public or employers. Individuals may also request a copy of their own criminal record from the Virginia State Police.

Criminal background checks in Virginia typically look back 7–10 years for most offenses.
