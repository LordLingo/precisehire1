### What is an Illinois Criminal Background Check and Why is it Important?

An Illinois criminal background check is an official process used to investigate an individual’s criminal history within the state of Illinois. This check typically involves searching for records related to arrests, convictions, pending charges, and other legal matters that may have been filed with law enforcement agencies across the state.

The primary purpose of a criminal background check is to ensure safety and security, especially when hiring employees, renting property, or making other important decisions that may require insight into a person’s past behavior. The check is comprehensive and often includes data from the Illinois State Police, local law enforcement, and courts.

#### What Information Does a Criminal Background Check Include?

Criminal background checks in Illinois typically include the following information:

  * **Arrest records** : Documented incidents where an individual was arrested or detained by law enforcement.
  * **Convictions** : Information about criminal convictions, including misdemeanor or felony charges, sentences, and penalties.
  * **Pending charges** : Any ongoing criminal cases or charges that have not yet been resolved.
  * **Probation or parole status** : Whether an individual is serving probation or parole after a conviction.

This information is used by employers, landlords, financial institutions, and other organizations to assess the potential risks associated with a person’s background.

### How to Request a Criminal Background Check in Illinois: A Step-by-Step Guide

Requesting a criminal background check in Illinois is a straightforward process, but it requires understanding the steps involved and knowing where to submit your request. In Illinois, the **Illinois State Police \(ISP\)** and local law enforcement agencies are responsible for providing criminal history checks. Depending on the situation, you can request either a statewide check or a local check.

Here’s a step-by-step guide on how to request a criminal background check:

  1. **Determine the Type of Check You Need**  
Decide if you need a **statewide** or **local** criminal background check. A statewide check provides a broader scope, pulling records from all counties within Illinois, while a local check focuses on a specific jurisdiction or county.
  2. **Gather the Required Information**  
To request a criminal background check in Illinois, you will need to provide the following details:

     * **Full Name** \(as it appears on official records\)
     * **Date of Birth** \(to help verify identity\)
     * **Address** \(current or past\)
     * **Fingerprints** \(for certain checks\)

These details are necessary to ensure the criminal history report is accurate and complete.
  3. **Choose the Request Method**  
In Illinois, you can request a criminal background check through several methods:

     * **Online** : The Illinois State Police offer an online system for submitting requests. This is often the quickest method and allows you to access results electronically.
     * **In-Person** : You can visit the Illinois State Police or local police department to submit your request in person. This method may require you to fill out forms and present identification.
     * **By Mail** : If you prefer to request the check via mail, you will need to download and complete the required forms, attach payment, and send them to the appropriate agency.
  4. **Pay the Fees**  
There are fees associated with obtaining a criminal background check in Illinois. Fees vary depending on the method of request and the type of check requested. Typically, the cost for a criminal record check is between $20 and $50, but this can vary. Payment can usually be made via credit card, check, or money order.
  5. **Wait for Results**  
After submitting the request and paying the fees, you will need to wait for the results. The time it takes to receive the report depends on the method used to request the check. Online requests typically provide results quickly, often within a few minutes to a few days, while mail requests may take several weeks.

### Legal Aspects of Criminal Background Checks in Illinois

![Legal Aspects of Criminal Background Checks in Illinois](https://precisehire.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Legal-Aspects-of-Criminal-Background-Checks-in-Illinois.png)

Criminal background checks are not only used to verify someone’s criminal history but are also governed by a set of laws and regulations to ensure fairness and compliance. In Illinois, there are several important legal considerations that individuals and businesses should be aware of when conducting criminal background checks.

**1\. Illinois Human Rights Act \(IHRA\)**  
The **Illinois Human Rights Act** protects individuals from discrimination based on criminal history in certain circumstances. For example, employers in Illinois cannot use criminal history as the sole reason for denying employment unless the criminal offense is directly related to the job. Employers must evaluate whether an applicant’s criminal history is relevant to the position they are applying for. Additionally, the law limits how far back an employer can consider criminal records in making decisions, typically not beyond 7 years for most types of employment.

**2\. Fair Credit Reporting Act \(FCRA\)**  
The **FCRA** is a federal law that regulates the collection, distribution, and use of consumer information, including criminal background data. Under this law, employers and other entities using criminal background checks for employment or other purposes must obtain the individual’s consent before conducting the check. They must also ensure that the information is accurate, and if they deny someone a position or access based on the background check, they are required to notify the individual and provide them with an opportunity to dispute any incorrect information.

**3\. Expungement and Sealing of Criminal Records**  
In Illinois, individuals may have the option to **expunge** or **seal** certain criminal records under specific circumstances. **Expungement** involves the complete removal of criminal records, making them unavailable to the public. **Sealing** a record means that it is hidden from public view but still accessible to law enforcement. These legal processes may affect the results of a criminal background check, especially for those seeking employment or housing. Individuals who have sealed or expunged records may not have their criminal history reflected in a background check, which can be beneficial for those who qualify.

**4\. Juvenile Records**  
Under Illinois law, juvenile criminal records are generally not available to the public, including employers and landlords. In some cases, juvenile records can be expunged or sealed when the individual reaches adulthood. As a result, these records typically do not show up on a standard background check.

Criminal background checks are not mandatory for all employment situations in Illinois. ###  __Can a criminal record in Illinois be expunged or sealed?

Yes, under certain conditions, a criminal record in Illinois can be expunged or sealed. Expungement removes the record completely, while sealing hides it from public view. The eligibility for expungement or sealing depends on factors such as the type of offense, the time passed since conviction, and whether the individual has complied with the terms of their sentence.
