### Introduction to Criminal Background Check in Hawaii

#### What is a Criminal Background Check?

A **criminal background check** is a process that involves reviewing an individual’s criminal history to assess whether they have been involved in any illegal activities. This check can reveal information such as arrests, convictions, pending charges, and, in some cases, dismissed charges. In Hawaii, criminal background checks are used for a variety of purposes, including employment, housing, and legal matters. They help employers, landlords, and other decision-makers ensure that individuals meet certain safety and legal standards before entering into agreements or hiring processes.

#### Why are Criminal Background Checks Important in Hawaii?

Criminal background checks are vital in Hawaii for several reasons. They help employers, landlords, and other organizations make informed decisions and minimize risks. For instance, they help employers assess whether an applicant’s criminal history might make them unsuitable for a job, especially if the position involves trust, security, or working with vulnerable populations. Landlords, on the other hand, use background checks to determine if potential tenants have a history of criminal activity that could affect the safety of the property or other residents.

Beyond employment and housing, criminal background checks also serve as a way for individuals to verify the accuracy of their records and ensure there are no errors or inaccuracies that could negatively impact their opportunities.

#### Types of Criminal Records Included in a Background Check

![Types of Criminal Records Included in a Background Check](https://precisehire.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Types-of-Criminal-Records-Included-in-a-Background-Check.png)

Criminal background checks typically cover various types of criminal records that help paint a complete picture of an individual’s history. The most common records included in a background check are:

#### 1\. **Felonies**

Felonies are serious crimes that typically involve harsher penalties, such as long-term imprisonment. Examples include violent offenses like assault and murder, as well as serious non-violent crimes like drug trafficking and fraud. Felony convictions are a significant factor in evaluating an individual’s background and often have a direct impact on their ability to secure certain jobs or housing.

#### 2\. **Misdemeanors**

Misdemeanors are less serious offenses compared to felonies and generally result in shorter sentences. Common misdemeanors include theft, vandalism, or minor drug offenses. Although they are not as severe as felonies, misdemeanors are still significant and can impact employment and housing decisions, especially if they are recent or relevant to the position being applied for.

#### 3\. **Arrests**

An arrest record indicates that a person was detained by law enforcement, though it does not imply guilt. Arrests alone do not necessarily indicate that the individual was convicted. However, arrests can be used by employers and landlords as a red flag, especially if the arrest was for a serious crime.

#### 4\. **Convictions**

A conviction occurs when a court of law finds an individual guilty of a criminal offense. Convictions are often the most important record in a background check as they are legally binding and have long-term consequences. Depending on the crime and its relevance to the job or housing opportunity, a conviction may impact an individual’s ability to gain employment or secure housing.

#### 5\. **Pending Charges**

In some cases, an individual may have pending charges that have not yet been resolved in court. These charges can appear on a background check and may influence hiring or housing decisions. However, pending charges do not equate to a conviction and may not be as heavily weighed in the decision-making process.

#### Legal Framework and Regulations Surrounding Criminal Background Checks in Hawaii

Hawaii has a set of laws and regulations that govern how criminal background checks are used, particularly for employment and tenant screening. These laws ensure that individuals’ rights are protected while allowing employers and landlords to make informed decisions.

#### Hawaii Fair Employment Practices Act

The **Hawaii Fair Employment Practices Act \(FEPA\)** prohibits employers from discriminating against job applicants based solely on their criminal history. However, an employer can consider an applicant’s criminal record if the offense directly relates to the duties of the position. Employers must also follow the **ban the box** law, which restricts them from asking about criminal history until later in the hiring process. This law is designed to prevent discrimination against individuals who have had run-ins with the law but have since rehabilitated.

#### Tenant Screening

For landlords, criminal background checks are an essential part of tenant screening. In Hawaii, landlords are allowed to review an applicant’s criminal history to determine whether they may pose a safety risk to other tenants or the property. However, landlords must be careful not to discriminate against potential tenants based on their criminal records unless there is a valid reason related to the safety or suitability of the tenant for the property.

### Legal Aspects of Criminal Background Checks in Hawaii

![Legal Aspects of Criminal Background Checks in Hawaii](https://precisehire.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Legal-Aspects-of-Criminal-Background-Checks-in-Hawaii.png)

These laws ensure that background checks are conducted fairly, transparently, and in a manner that minimizes the risk of discrimination. Below are the key legal aspects to consider when conducting a criminal background check in Hawaii.

* * *

#### **Hawaii Fair Employment Practices Act**

The Hawaii Fair Employment Practices Act protects job applicants and employees from discrimination based on their criminal history unless it is directly relevant to the job. Employers are required to take a fair and reasonable approach, considering factors such as:

  * The nature of the offense.
  * How much time has passed since the conviction or sentence completion.
  * The relationship between the conviction and the responsibilities of the job.

Employers must assess these factors individually for each applicant or employee rather than applying blanket disqualifications. This ensures fairness and reduces the risk of overlooking qualified candidates due to unrelated criminal history.

* * *

#### **Ban the Box Law in Hawaii**

Hawaii’s Ban the Box law is one of the most robust in the United States. Under this law, employers are prohibited from inquiring about an applicant’s criminal record until after a conditional job offer is made. This allows applicants to be judged on their qualifications and skills rather than their criminal history during the initial stages of the hiring process.

Ban the Box laws apply to public and private employers in Hawaii, and violations can result in fines or other penalties. Employers must ensure they follow these regulations to avoid legal consequences and maintain a fair hiring process.

* * *

#### **Criminal Records in Tenant Screening**

For landlords, conducting criminal background checks is a common step in screening potential tenants. However, Hawaii law requires landlords to approach this process fairly and transparently. Landlords must:

  * Notify applicants in writing that a criminal background check will be conducted.
  * Obtain the applicant’s written consent before proceeding.
  * Provide a copy of the background check report if the results lead to denial of the application.

Similar to employment decisions, landlords should avoid blanket disqualifications based on criminal history. Instead, they should evaluate whether the record is relevant to the safety of other tenants or the property.

* * *

#### **Compliance with the Fair Credit Reporting Act \(FCRA\)**

When employers or landlords use third-party services to conduct background checks, they are required to comply with the Fair Credit Reporting Act \(FCRA\). The FCRA establishes clear guidelines to protect individuals during the screening process:

  1. **Notification:** Individuals must be informed that a background check will be conducted.
  2. **Authorization:** Written consent must be obtained before proceeding with the check.
  3. **Adverse Action Procedures:** If an adverse decision is made based on the results, individuals must be provided with a copy of the report and a summary of their rights under the FCRA.
