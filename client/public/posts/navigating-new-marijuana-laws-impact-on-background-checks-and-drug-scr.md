### Understanding California’s AB 2188

Starting in 2024, California’s Assembly Bill 2188 \(AB 2188\) will significantly alter the hiring landscape in the state by prohibiting employers from testing applicants or employees for marijuana use outside of work unless they can substantiate that such use impairs job performance. This marks a paradigm shift in employment practices, reflecting a growing recognition of the need for workplace inclusivity and respect for individual choices regarding marijuana use.

#### Key Provisions of AB 2188

  * **Prohibition on Testing** : Employers in California will not be allowed to test current or prospective employees for marijuana use outside work hours, which underscores the importance of considering an individual’s performance rather than personal lifestyle choices. [Source](<https://www.calaborlaw.com/the-2024-california-law-on-marijuana-testing-of-employees-and-job-applicants-ab-2188/>).
  * **Exceptions to the Rule** : This law is not absolute — positions in specific fields, such as the building and construction trades or roles requiring federal background checks, are exceptions where testing remains permissible [Source](<https://www.calaborlaw.com/the-2024-california-law-on-marijuana-testing-of-employees-and-job-applicants-ab-2188/>).
  * **Anti-Discrimination Protections** : Employers cannot discriminate against employees or applicants based solely on marijuana use outside of work unless there’s proof of impairment during work hours [Source](<https://www.calaborlaw.com/the-2024-california-law-on-marijuana-testing-of-employees-and-job-applicants-ab-2188/>).

### The Broader Context of Marijuana Legislation

The recent changes in California are part of a broader trend across the United States, with thirty-three states and the District of Columbia legalizing medical marijuana [Source](<https://www.goodhire.com/blog/video-are-you-up-to-date-on-marijuana-laws-in-your-state/>). Eleven states have instituted measures that prevent employers from making adverse employment decisions based solely on positive drug test results for medical marijuana users.

#### Expunging Past Convictions

Cities across the nation are taking proactive steps to expunge or seal past marijuana convictions, which will change the data available during background checks and ensure applicants are judged based on their current abilities and character rather than outdated offenses [Source](<https://www.goodhire.com/blog/video-are-you-up-to-date-on-marijuana-laws-in-your-state/>).

### Implications for Background Checks and Drug Screening Policies

#### Moving Away from Zero-Tolerance Policies

The recruitment industry has seen a shift away from zero-tolerance drug policies that often led to discrimination claims against employers. Many potential employees have been denied opportunities due to medical marijuana use, despite having valid prescriptions. Compliance with evolving state laws is now paramount in avoiding such legal pitfalls [Source](<https://www.goodhire.com/blog/video-are-you-up-to-date-on-marijuana-laws-in-your-state/>).

#### Ensuring Compliance

Different states have varying regulations regarding drug screenings and employee protections regarding marijuana use. For instance, in states like Arizona and Illinois, penalizing individuals simply for holding a medical marijuana card is against the law [Source](<https://www.goodhire.com/blog/video-are-you-up-to-date-on-marijuana-laws-in-your-state/>). Employers must stay informed and adapt their policies accordingly to mitigate risks of discrimination claims.

### Navigating Federal Regulations

While state laws are progressing, federal laws still classify marijuana as a controlled substance, complicating matters for employers. Many are caught between state protections and the federal classification of marijuana, which means recruitment processes must be carefully designed to comply with both sets of laws [Source](<https://www.accusourcehr.com/blog/how-federal-marijuana-laws-impact-background-checks>).

#### Best Practices for Employers

  * **Regular Policy Review** : Regularly update drug screening policies in accordance with state and federal laws.
  * **Uniform Testing Procedures** : Ensure drug testing is conducted uniformly across all applicants, maintaining fairness and compliance, as mandated by California law [Source](<https://californiacannabis.org/drug-testing-law>).
  * **Training HR Teams** : Equip HR professionals with understanding and knowledge of current marijuana laws to foster an inclusive hiring environment while ensuring compliance.
